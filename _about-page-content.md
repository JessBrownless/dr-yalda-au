# About page — rewired content

Working draft. First person throughout. Each section is labelled with its IA role so you can think about layout separately from copy.

---

## 1. Hero (tight intro)

**Replaces:** current hero card body
**Role:** identity + signature expertise + reason to keep reading
**Voice:** first person, two sentences max

> Overline — *The story so far*
> H1 — *About Dr Yalda Jamali*
> Body — I'm a Sydney cosmetic doctor recognised for facial balancing and contouring. After ten years in the field — five of those alongside world-renowned specialists — my practice is built on personalised, evidence-based care, with refined results that feel like you.

Notes:
- Two sentences instead of the current six-claim run-on
- Credentials intentionally stripped out — they live in their own section below
- Signature expertise (facial balancing and contouring) and experience signal (ten years, world-renowned specialists) are the two pieces that earn their place here

- This section is now the *only* place credentials appear, so the user reads each qualification exactly once

## 3. A friendly hello *(new)*

**New section.** Friendly, personal interlude. Text-image layout.
**Role:** humanise the page. Give the user a glimpse of who Dr Yalda is as a person and why she does this work, before the page transitions into philosophy. Sits between the formal Credentials block and the poetic parallax quote, warming up the tone in between.

> Overline — *A note from me*
> Heading — *It's almost never about looking different.*
> Body — I came to cosmetic medicine through dermatology, and what's kept me here isn't the procedures — it's the conversations. Most of the people I see aren't asking to change their face; they're asking to feel more like themselves in it. That's the work I'm here for.

Optional second paragraph (adds lifestyle warmth — needs a real biographical detail from Dr Yalda to land authentically):

> Outside the clinic, I'm [a runner / a mother / a quiet reader / etc.]. I think the work is better when it's grounded in a life beyond it.

Notes:
- This is the only section on the page where the heading is a *line of speech* rather than a label. That's deliberate — it signals warmth and personal voice the instant the user scans the page
- Layout candidate: text-image, with a softer, more candid portrait than the polished hero shot. The hero already has the editorial moment — this one should feel like meeting her at a café. Image-right works since the hero is image-left, giving the page a left-right-left rhythm
- Keep this section *short*. One paragraph is the sweet spot; two is the maximum. Brevity is part of what makes the warmth land — over-writing here flips the tone back into marketing copy
- If there's a "Yalda" signature SVG or handwritten note, this is the place to use it — sits below the paragraph as a sign-off and reinforces the personal voice

## 5. Values

**Replaces:** current values section
**Role:** the 'how I think about it' content. Same 01–04 structure, voice flipped to first person to match the rest of the page

> Overline — *Values*

01 / Honesty
Every consultation is grounded in transparency. I'll always tell you what is — and isn't — right for you, even if that means recommending nothing at all.

02 / Precision
I take a meticulous, considered approach to every treatment. Results are refined, never overdone — shaped by anatomy, not trend.

Every recommendation is backed by clinical evidence and ongoing education. No hype, no shortcuts — just what the science supports.

I build long-term relationships through consistent, honest care. The goal is always to earn your confidence with results that feel like you.

Notes:

- The "shaped by anatomy, not trend" line in Precision is doing a lot of work — keep it

## 6. What to expect *(new, optional)*

**New section.** Fills the gap between abstract values and 'book now.'
**Role:** concrete, no-pressure, anti-anxiety. Tells a nervous first-time patient exactly what's about to happen if they book

> Overline — *What to expect*
> Heading — *Your first consultation, simply.*

01 / Conversation
We start with a conversation — what's brought you in, what you're hoping to feel like, and what you'd rather avoid. There's no pressure to commit to anything in the first visit.

02 / Assessment
I assess your face holistically — proportion, anatomy, and skin health, rather than treating areas in isolation. If there's nothing to do today, I'll say so.

03 / A plan that's yours
You leave with a clear, written plan — what's recommended, what it costs, and what order to approach it in. Whether you act on it with me or elsewhere is up to you.

Notes:
- Mirrors the 01–04 numbering pattern from Values, so the two sections feel like a paired set visually
- Every line is designed to reduce a specific friction: "no pressure" (commitment anxiety), "if there's nothing to do today I'll say so" (upsell anxiety), "whether you act on it with me or elsewhere is up to you" (lock-in anxiety)
- This is the section to skip if you want to keep the page lean — but it's also the highest-converting kind of content for a doctor at this price point. Worth raising with Dr Yalda

## 7. CTA

**Replaces:** current "Feeling overwhelmed?" CTA
**Role:** match the emotional state of someone at the *end* of an About page, not a Services page

> Overline — *Want to meet first?*
> Heading — *Complimentary 15-minute online consultation.*
> Body — Not ready to book a full appointment? Have a 15-minute video call with me to talk through your concerns and explore your options — no pressure, no commitment.
> Button — *Book the 15-minute call*

Notes:
- "Feeling overwhelmed?" assumes the user is staring at a treatment menu. Here, they've just finished reading about her — the right emotion is "I want to meet her before I commit." "Want to meet first?" frames the same offer for that state
- Button copy is more specific ("Book the 15-minute call" vs "Book a consultation") which usually lifts click-through on a soft-commit CTA. Use whichever feels better

---

## Final order

1. Hero — tight intro
2. Credentials — consolidated
3. A friendly hello *(new)*
4. Parallax quote
5. Values
6. What to expect *(optional)*
7. CTA — meet-first framing

Seven sections, no duplicated facts, consistent first-person voice, and a cleaner emotional arc: identity → credibility → human → philosophy → approach → practical → invitation. The "friendly hello" is the warming element the page was missing — it gives the user a moment to meet the person before the page asks them to commit.
